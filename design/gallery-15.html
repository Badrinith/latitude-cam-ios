Latitude Cam / Gallery, rebuilt / Fifteen directions

Not a grid with a viewer bolted on — fifteen different ideas of what a roll of film is.

The broken gallery was a photo-app grid wearing the app's colours. These fifteen start from what the app already is — a camera with a film shelf, sprockets, a loupe, a light table — and ask what a gallery for that object looks like. Each pairs a gallery structure with its own edit entry point, noted below the mockup rather than built as a second full screen fifteen times over.

What I'd actually build

01

Contact roll — the honest default

It's the same object the film barrel already is: sprockets, a strip, one continuous surface. It reuses the visual language you already trust in this app rather than inventing a new one, and a filmstrip scales to any roll length without redesigning. This is what I'd ship first.

09

Archive drawers — if organisation matters more than browsing

The family grouping already exists in the two-tier film selector; drawers are that same idea turned into furniture. Best for someone with hundreds of frames who thinks "where are my Rust shots" before "show me everything."

05

Negative strip — the boldest, and worth prototyping

No camera app does this. It's true to what the sensor actually produced before any processing, and the tap-to-flip-positive gesture doubles as the transition into editing rather than needing a separate "open" action. Higher risk: negatives read as broken images to anyone unfamiliar with film.

15

Cascade is the one I'd avoid

Handsome, but it's the same masonry every photo app already runs. Least specific to what this app is.

Each mockup uses the app's shipped tokens — Ink, Tone, Accent, the eleven film swatches, SF Mono for data, a serif for headers matching the editor's own headers. Nothing here is built. Say a number (or two — gallery and edit can come from different rows) and I'll build it against the real PhotoGallery/Photos-backed data, fixing the load-order bug in the same pass.
