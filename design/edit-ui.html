Latitude Cam / Editor / Ten to pick from

Sixteen controls, and one thumb.

The editor now carries exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, black point, temperature, tint, saturation, vibrance, structure, sharpen, eleven stocks, intensity, three effects and a crop. That is more than a tab bar and two sliders can hold. These ten each answer the same question differently — how many controls should be visible at once, and how much of the picture may they cover to do it.

If you want my read

02

Barrel bank is the one I would build

Every other direction introduces a control the app does not otherwise have. This one is the barrel you already ship, at a smaller size — the same drag, the same detent, the same haptic as the shutter and film barrels. An editor should not need to be learned separately from the camera it belongs to. Its weakness is real though: barrels show values, not names, so the group heading has to carry the labelling.

06

Split compare is the one worth stealing from

Not necessarily as the whole design, but as a behaviour any of the others could adopt. Judging an edit against a remembered original is the thing people are worst at, and a permanent seam fixes it for the cost of a drag handle.

01

Console if the priority is that nothing hides

Five labelled faders with live values, no discovery required. It is the densest of the ten and the least characterful, which for a tool used in a hurry may be the right trade.

09

Fan is the one I would avoid

It gives the picture the most room by making every control invisible until touched. Beautiful in a demo; a user who does not know to press and hold sees an editor with no controls at all.

Drawn with the app's own tokens. The photograph in each is drawn, not shot, and carries a warm lamp, a cool window, foliage and skin so a colour control has somewhere to show itself. Nothing here is built — the editor currently ships as a tab bar with sliders, which is closest to 01. Give me a number.
