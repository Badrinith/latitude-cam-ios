Latitude Cam / Film selector / Ten to pick from

Eleven names, and a roll too narrow to hold them.

VERMILION and PORCELAIN do not fit a 78pt segment, and no amount of tracking will make them. A horizontal barrel gives each name the width of one segment; these ten each find that width somewhere else — down the side, on a card, in a window, or by asking for the family first and the stock second. All are quiet until touched and all print the family under the name.

02

Two-tier is the one I would build

It is the only direction that still works at twenty stocks. Everything else here solves eleven; this solves the shape of the problem, and it reuses the barrel you already have rather than introducing a second kind of control. The cost is a second gesture to reach a stock in another family.

05

Slug line if you want it to disappear

The quietest of the ten at rest — one line of serif type over the picture and nothing else. It is also the least mechanical, which cuts against the rest of the app's chrome.

03

Card index is the most antique

Aged card stock and a serif face, fanning as you reach in. It reads as a thing you own rather than a control, and the category has a natural home under the name. Only three are visible at once, so it wants pairing with the family tier.

09

Contact sheet is the honest answer, at a price

It is the only one that shows what each stock will do to the scene in front of you. Eleven live renders is real work — the existing preview machinery does four at 4Hz — so it wants measuring before committing.

Drawn with the app's own tokens, with a serif face where the design calls for something older than SF Mono. The stage behind each is drawn, not photographed; the white disc is the release, at scale, so you can see what each design leaves clear. Nothing is built — give me a number.
