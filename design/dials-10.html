Ten ways to have fewer dials

Six dials is a control panel, not a camera. But the answer isn't to delete controls — it's that real cameras never had six independent ones either. They used mechanisms that made settings depend on each other.

Two are worth stealing. In the 1950s the shutter maker Friedrich Deckel introduced EV coupling: shutter and aperture locked together, so one number set the exposure and moving either ring moved the other to compensate. In 1960 the ASA standard formalised APEX — Av + Tv = Bv + Sv — exposure as a sum, where any three terms determine the fourth.

So: four exposure controls were never four decisions. They were one decision and a choice about which trade to make. Each concept below is a different way to expose that.

WHAT WE HAVE NOW

Six equal dials in a scrolling rail — aperture, ISO, shutter, white balance, exposure, focus — each independent, each the same weight. Nothing tells you that three of them are the same decision, and the rail is wider than the phone.

01

The Yoke

One dial sets how much light. A second, smaller one walks through the shutter/aperture pairs that deliver it. Deckel's coupling, made explicit: brightness and trade-off are separate questions, so they get separate knobs.

REPLACES
aperture · shutter · exposure comp
DIALS
2 (+ ISO on auto)
COST
You can't set an exact pair without walking to it.
02

The Balance

APEX drawn as a beam. Each setting is a weight on a scale that must level against the light the meter reads. Drag one and the others counterweight. The relationship stops being something you memorise and becomes something you watch.

REPLACES
the whole exposure group
DIALS
0 — four draggable weights
COST
Novel enough to need teaching once.
03

The Collar

One knob, ringed by a mode collar. The collar decides what the knob drives; the knob is always in the same place under the same thumb. Muscle memory learns one location instead of six.

REPLACES
every dial, one at a time
DIALS
1 + a collar
COST
Two gestures to change two things.
04

Concentric

Aperture outside, shutter inside, on one axis. Locked, they turn together and exposure holds while the character changes. Press the hub to break the link and set one alone. Two settings, one place on the glass.

REPLACES
aperture + shutter
DIALS
1 ring pair
COST
Two concentric targets needs a big dial.
05

The Slide Rule

Two cursors on one logarithmic track. The distance between them is the exposure. Slide the pair to change brightness, slide one to change the trade. The arithmetic is the layout, so nothing has to be computed.

REPLACES
aperture · shutter · EV
DIALS
0 — two cursors
COST
Wants width; awkward turned sideways.
06

The Zone

No exposure dials at all. A tone strip from black to white; you say where the subject should sit and the camera solves the rest. Adams' zone system as the only control — it asks the question a photographer actually has.

REPLACES
the entire exposure group
DIALS
0 — one placement
COST
Gives up deliberate motion blur.
07

The Crown

One dial that changes what it holds. Press the crown and it steps to the next setting, the way a watch crown pulls out to hours, then date. The dots say how deep you are. Depth instead of width.

REPLACES
the whole rail
DIALS
1
COST
Hidden state — you must look to know.
08

The Band

Aperture stops being a number. You drag the near and far edges of what should be sharp, and the f-stop follows. The lens barrel's depth-of-field scale, run backwards — state the intent, let the setting be the consequence.

REPLACES
aperture + focus
DIALS
0 — one band
COST
Can ask for a depth no lens can give.
09

Two Thumbs

Exactly two controls, where the thumbs already rest. Left is how bright, right is how it looks. Everything else runs itself until you hold a dial down, and then its group unfolds under your finger.

REPLACES
the rail, by demoting it
DIALS
2 visible
COST
Manual work is always one step further.
10

The Ledger

The readout is the control. One line of type states what the camera is doing; touch any number in it and scrub that number in place. Nothing is added to the frame, because the thing you'd have added was already there.

REPLACES
every dial
DIALS
0
COST
Small targets; least tactile of the ten.
