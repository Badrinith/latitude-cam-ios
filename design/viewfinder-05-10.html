05 + 10, working together

The Edge Meter takes the one thing people change constantly — how bright — and gives it a full-length target down the side of the frame. The Strip takes everything else and reduces it to a line of type that only becomes a control when you touch it.

Together they cover the whole exposure group with nothing covering the picture: one thumb slide for brightness, one tap for anything else.

THE RULE THAT DECIDES THE LANDSCAPE LAYOUT

The meter follows gravity. The release follows the screen.

Brighter must always be physically up, or the gesture stops being obvious the moment you turn the phone — so the meter moves to whichever edge is vertical in your hand. The release does the opposite and never moves, because a shutter you have to hunt for is worse than one at an odd angle. The Strip sits with the release, and only its lettering turns.

WHAT THIS REPLACES

Six dials, the PRO button, the dial rail and the barrel readout — all of it — with a strip that is 22pt tall and a meter 26pt wide. Focus peaking and grid stay in the top switches; manual focus becomes a token in the strip like the rest.

The honest cost: a token is a smaller target than a 70pt dial, and nothing here is knurled metal. This trades the pleasure of the object for the picture being visible. That is a real trade, and worth seeing on the phone before deciding.
