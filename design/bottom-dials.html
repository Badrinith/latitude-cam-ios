Latitude Cam / Bottom dials / Ten to pick from

Everything a thumb needs, above the shutter.

Nothing sits in the middle of the frame any more. The dials live in the band between the shutter button and the bottom of the picture — inside the arc a thumb actually sweeps, shown dashed on each phone. Collapsed they are chips. One tap and the chosen one inflates into a real dial, focused, with the others stepping back. Each direction below also shows what it becomes in landscape, since the cluster has to migrate to the short edge when the body turns.

Turning the body

The demo runs on a loop. The body turns; the cluster travels with the shutter to the short edge, and every engraved value counter-rotates so it never ends up sideways.

SETTINGS
1/125
PRO

The chrome turns, the picture does not

The preview never rotates — a camera's finder shows the world the right way up whichever way you hold the body. Only the controls move.

Values counter-rotate

Each engraved number spins the opposite way at the same rate, so it stays readable through the whole transition rather than arriving upright at the end.

420ms, one spring

Migration and counter-rotation run on a single spring so the cluster arrives as one object. Two timings would make it look like parts falling into place.

An open dial stays open

Rotating mid-adjustment keeps the control live and the value unchanged. Losing your place because you turned the phone would be its own small betrayal.

If you want my read

You asked for ten to choose from, so these are only leanings.

03

Half moon is the one I would build

It gives the largest control for the least covered picture, because half the dial is understood to continue below the screen. It is also the only one that rotates into landscape without redrawing — the same half-disc simply attaches to the right edge instead of the bottom.

04

Barrel if the gesture worries you most

The dials that failed did so invisibly. A knurled barrel is the only shape here that says "drag me sideways" without a label, which is worth a lot when the last control's problem was that nobody could see why it did not respond.

07

Bezel arc is the interesting risk

Putting the values on the exact path a thumb sweeps is the most ergonomically correct answer and the least familiar one. Worth prototyping before committing — it either feels obvious in the hand or not at all.

09

Tray is the one I would avoid

It is the closest thing here to the bottom sheet you asked me to get rid of. Handsome, but it covers a third of the frame to show one number.

All ten use the app's tokens from Theme.swift — they differ in geometry and gesture, not palette. The dashed arc on each phone is a 280pt sweep struck from the shutter: everything inside it is reachable without shifting grip. The scene behind the glass is drawn, not photographed. Nothing here is built — give me a number and I will build that one.
