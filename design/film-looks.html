Latitude Cam / Film looks / Ten to pick from

Ten stocks, in four families.

Not ten unrelated presets. Reversal films are saturated and contrasty because a slide is the final object; print films are flat and forgiving because a negative is only the middle of the process. Grouping them that way is what makes a set navigable — you reach for a family first and a stock second. Every frame below is the same drawn scene under the real matrix each look would ship with, so the differences are the ones you would actually get.

What this changes in the build

01

Six new stocks, four kept

Amber, Slate, Rust and Mono stay exactly as they are and get sorted into families. Vermilion, Meridian, Porcelain, Harbour, Ledger and Ash are new. Neutral remains outside the set as the default — it is the absence of a look, not one of them.

02

The matrix alone will not carry six of them

Vermilion, Porcelain, Ledger, Mono and Ash all depend on a tone curve as much as on channel gains, and `filmVectors` is a 3×3 with no curve. Either the profile grows a gamma term, or those six arrive as approximations of themselves. I would add the curve.

03

Eleven stocks will not fit one barrel

At 96pt per name the film barrel already spans four. Eleven means either a much longer roll to drag through, or the family becomes the first choice and the stock the second — which is the argument for grouping them in the first place.

04

Names are original

Every stock here is named for what it does, not for what it resembles. The reference labels — Landscape, Portrait, Overcast, Cine — say what each is for without borrowing anyone's trademark.

Each frame is the same drawn scene through the actual matrix the look would ship with, applied via feColorMatrix in sRGB — the same maths CIColorMatrix runs, so these are previews rather than impressions. The scene is drawn, not photographed. Nothing is built: tell me which stocks you want and whether the profile should gain a tone curve.
